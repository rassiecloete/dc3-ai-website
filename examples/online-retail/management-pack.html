Cycle 20 is projected from seven days; tested over 35 closed cycles, the first-week cost per sale landed on average within 1% of the close, with no lean either way.

Gross contribution cycle 19 gave back 10% on last year; cycle 20 is projecting ahead

Cycle 19: $251K, 10% below last year’s $277K (last year’s strongest cycle), on 5% more orders. Ad spend of $180K against $116K absorbed the growth. Cycle 20 projects $268K against $213K last year, from seven days, against a dear cycle last year.

A caveat we state rather than hide: labour is charged as a fixed share of every order, as if it were fully variable. Much of it is fixed in the short run, so this line penalises a strong cycle and flatters a weak one. Read the trend, not the single cycle.

Basis how every number is counted

Orders are counted once per customer checkout, from the order system, and reconciled to the accounts. Credited sales use our own attribution rule on raw click journeys (first and last click weighted, brand clicks discounted, a 90-day click window); the ad platforms’ own conversion counts are not used. Cost per sale = spend ÷ credited sales over the window, read on the tracker’s shape: the last three cycles against the six before and against last year, coloured only beyond the measure’s own usual swing. Break-even = full cost per order including labour and shipping. Gross contribution = orders × each product’s contribution per order before advertising, minus all ad spend, two-product orders counted once.
