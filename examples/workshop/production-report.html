Production, reported every morning

Before the workshop opens, the owner gets one page on their phone: what went out yesterday, what came in, whether that was a good day, how long customers are waiting and where the backlog stands. Every figure comes from the help desk, the timesheets and the books, joined and checked.

  • The answer first: a good day or not, and why
  • Output per technician hour, so a short-staffed day is read fairly
  • Timesheet gaps flagged by name, before payroll
  • Wait times per product line, so customers are given honest dates

How these are counted one definition per number, the same every day

Job = an order finished for the first time, as recorded on the help desk. A ticket reopened later is never counted twice. Warranty return = re-work on a returned unit: extra work, never inside jobs. New orders = paid orders from the books by order date, one per order; cancelled orders and shipping-only charges never count. Activity = a help-desk task closed by a technician; late = closed after its due date. Technician hours = hours on the payroll timesheets, leave and holidays excluded; a day with no hours and no leave booked is flagged by name. Wait time = working days from the unit arriving to the job being finished, weekends and company holidays skipped.
